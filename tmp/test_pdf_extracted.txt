Abstract8
Standard target speaker extraction (TSE) models are trained on a wide corpus of noise and9
may not be optimally suited for car noise. Furthermore, such models perform speaker separation10
alone and does not determine if the target speaker is active, listen for wake words, perform voice11
to action mapping. Hence this work presents a full pipeline utilizing a custom TSE model trained12
on car cabin noise, followed by target-speech presence gating, wake word detection, intent map-13
ping and intent to action mapping. This would provide a full end to end pipeline for detecting,14
extracting and understanding the intent of the target speaker, preventing other passengers from15
accidental trigger of wake words and accurate mapping of voice audio to actions. The proposed16
work presents a multi stage model, utilizing SpEx+ based TSE adapted using car cabin noise,17
a target-speech presence gate, wake-word detection, intent classification, and intent-to-action18
mapping. Datasets were used for each individual stage in the pipeline ( LibriSpeech, Libri2Mix,19
CAVEMOVE, AISHELL-5, Google Speech Commands V2, Fluent Speech Commands, Vehicle20
Interior Sound Dataset). On the reported CAVEMOVE evaluation, extraction improves mean21
SI-SDR by 10.98 dB. The intent classifier reaches 99.31% top-1 accuracy on the clean Fluent22
Speech Commands test set.23
1 Introduction24
As compared to standard development of speech models where clean, anechoic, close-talk speech is25
used as data, real scenarios of voice interfaces in vehicles operate in a substantially different environ-26
ment. Road and other vehicle noise, ventilation, reverberation, loudspeaker playback, microphone27
placement, and speech from other passengers in the vehicle add to the unwanted signal noise cap-28
tured by on-device microphones. Simulating such scenarios would require hundreds of hours of in29
car audio recordings, and thus would prove difficult in data acquisition. Recent in-car corpora such30
as AISHELL-5 [1] and CAVEMOVE [2] make such systematic data acquisition and research more31
accessible. AISHELL-5 provides multichannel in-car speech and noise, and Chinese audio tracks,32
1\nwhich we do not use as our focus is primarily on English speech and data. CAVEMOVE provides33
vehicle impulse responses and noise recordings that support the ease of synthesis of cabin mixtures34
for the development of a robust in-car model. Due to the nature of the various source of noises, it35
is highlighted that a model trained on generic speech mixtures may not generalize well to car cabin36
noise mixtures.37
There are several tasks that a in-car voice assistant must solve, extending beyond simple acoustic38
separation. Given a reference target speaker, the model should retain the target speaker’s speech39
while suppressing competing passenger speech and background noise using accurate masks on the40
mixture, determine when the target is speaking, detect a keyword ( such as ’Marvin’ in our case)41
phrase, interpret the command and map the command to the inferred action. Hence unlike a42
standard model, our proposed work identifies several distinct questions: who is speaking, whether43
the intended user is speaking now, whether the system should activate, and what action is requested44
from the user. This pipeline is quintessential as a passenger should not be able to use the wake45
words and activate a driver-oriented command path. Hence speaker diarization is important and46
proves as a vital stage in intent mapping for determining speaker activity over time [3].47
Target speaker extraction (TSE) proves useful in this setting. Unlike speaker-independent sepa-48
ration ( such as Blind Source Separation paradigms), which estimates a set of sources without the49
understanding of which speaker is mapped to what source, TSE provides a reference utterance to50
condition the separation on the target speaker. SpEx+ is a time-domain TSE architecture that51
uses a shared representation for mixture and reference speech and attained strong results on stan-52
dard speech-mixture benchmarks [4]. While it has attained success on generic and synthetically53
mixed speech, it is not implicitly guaranteed that the model will have high performance on in-car54
cabin noise mixtures. Due to a domain mismatch, there is motivation to evaluate and adapt such55
architectures to in-car recordings from generic training mixtures. LibriSpeech and Libri2Mix offer56
clean speech and speech-mixtures resources for model development, while CAVEMOVE and vehicle-57
interior recordings contribute driving noise and cabin acoustics [2, 5–7]. AISHELL-5 further provides58
real multichannel, multi-speaker in-car recordings for speech technology research [1].59
The successive stages in the pipeline also require task-specific data and evaluation sets. Keyword60
spotting (KWS) detects a short, predefined phrase in an audio stream in real time; the Google61
Speech Commands V2 corpus was introduced as a benchmark for such keyword spotting and limited-62
vocabulary recognition tasks [8]. Spoken language understanding maps an utterance to a predefined63
and structured intent, and the Fluent Speech Commands corpus supports evaluation of direct speech-64
to-intent systems [9]. However, even though isolated and clean intent test sets perform well in65
accuracy, it does not measure the performance of the entire system, as it does not consider the66
separation of the mixture, the selection of the target speaker, whether the activation phrase was67
accepted correctly, or whether the entire system pipeline executed the correct intended action. It is68
therefore useful to describe, evaluate and assess the system, by stage and as a cascade.69
This work presents a multi-stage in-car voice-processing pipeline utilizing a SpEx+-based target70
speaker extractor adapted using vehicle-cabin noise augmented mixtures with a reference utterance.71
The extracted signal is then passed through a target-speech presence gate, wake-word detection,72
intent classification, and intent-to-action mapping. The stages utilize datasets suited to their respec-73
2\ntive stages: LibriSpeech and Libri2Mix for speech and mixture generation; and the CAVEMOVE,74
AISHELL-5, and the Vehicle Interior Sound Dataset for cabin acoustics, noise, and evaluation or75
augmentation of mixtures. Google Speech Commands V2 for keyword spotting. Fluent Speech76
Commands for intent classification. The pipeline is designed to make the activation of the intent77
detector and action mapper conditional on the target speaker, reducing the risk of other passenger78
triggering an action.79
On the reported CAVEMOVE evaluation, the extraction stage improves mean SI-SDR by 10.9880
dB relative to the in-car noisy mixture input. The intent classifier achieves 99.31% top-1 accuracy81
on the clean Fluent Speech Commands test set. These results demonstrate promising performance82
for two individual stages considered independently; they should not be interpreted as an end-to-end83
voice-to-action accuracy. The contribution of this work is the construction and stage-wise evaluation84
of a in-vehicle cabin-adapted, target-conditioned voice pipeline that connects extraction to target-85
speech gating, wake-word detection, intent recognition, and action mapping. The remainder of86
this paper details the full corpora of datasets and methods, reporting stage-specific and pipeline87
evaluations, and discusses the limitations and requirements for validating the complete system under88
real in-car use and production grade scenarios.89

2 Related Work90
This section builds from trainable neural networks to the four decisions in the in-car pipeline. It91
distinguishes established methods used as foundations from the implementation choices made in this92
repository. The stages are trained separately and connected at inference; the system is therefore a93
staged cascade, not a single jointly optimized end-to-end neural network.94
2.1 Neural Networks, Objectives, and Optimization95
Neural networks learn a parameterized mapping by repeatedly comparing predictions with a task-96
specific target and propagating the resulting gradient through the layers. Back-propagation supplies97
these derivatives; Adam estimates adaptive first and second moments; AdamW separates weight de-98
cay from the gradient-based update; gradient clipping limits unstable update norms; cosine schedules99
3\nreduce the learning rate over training; and label smoothing replaces one-hot targets with softened100
targets in multiclass learning [10–15]. These are complementary pieces of a training procedure rather101
than interchangeable losses. The repository uses AdamW across the principal training stages, uses102
gradient clipping in its configured training scripts, applies cosine decay to the KWS and wav2vec103
intent recipes, and uses label smoothing of 0.03 in the wav2vec intent fine-tuning script.104

learn local patterns with shared filters; batch normalization stabilizes intermediate feature scales;129
recurrent networks such as LSTMs maintain a gated state across frames; and Transformers use130
self-attention to relate positions over a sequence. Self-supervised wav2vec 2.0 pretraining provides131
5\ncontextual speech representations that can be fine-tuned for recognition with a smaller task-specific132
head [16–21]. This progression explains the repository’s compact log-mel CNN for one-second wake-133
word classification and its pretrained wav2vec 2.0 encoder for 31-class intent recognition. A CRNN134
intent baseline is also implemented, but the PDF’s reported best intent model is wav2vec 2.0, not135
Word2Vec.136

2.3 Target-Speaker Extraction and Target-Presence Decisions153
Target-speaker extraction (TSE) uses an enrollment utterance to select a speaker from an acoustic154
mixture. VoiceFilter conditions spectrogram masking on a speaker representation; SpeakerBeam155
connects target-speaker adaptation to recognition; SpEx+ performs time-domain extraction at mul-156
tiple temporal resolutions; and Direction-Aware Speaker Beam incorporates multichannel spatial157
evidence. Target-speaker VAD, enrollment-robust extraction, target-absent training, false-alarm158
control, and target-confusion analysis address complementary failure modes [3, 4, 22–28]. AISHELL-159
5 and CAVEMOVE add real or simulated vehicle conditions for which generic mixtures alone are160
insufficient [1, 2, 5–7]. These methods motivate a cabin-adapted extractor followed by an explicit161
presence decision; extraction quality and confidence that the desired person is speaking are separate162
quantities.163

2.4 Wake-Word Detection, Intent, and the Cascade197
Keyword spotting (KWS) recognizes a predefined phrase under strict latency, compute, and false-198
alarm constraints. Compact CNNs and Speech Commands establish small-footprint keyword-recognition199
baselines; VoiceFilter-Lite studies a related streaming target-speaker front end, while Fluent Speech200
Commands and SLURP support fixed-domain and broader spoken-language-understanding evalua-201
tion. Textual knowledge distillation and wav2vec 2.0 represent alternative approaches to learning202
intent from speech [8, 9, 20, 21, 29–31]. In this repository, Stage 3 is a binary Marvin/non-Marvin203
log-mel CNN, and Stage 4 is a 31-class wav2vec 2.0 intent classifier. Their separate training sets204
and clean-set scores do not, by themselves, measure whether a target user can traverse the full205
wake-to-action cascade.206

2.5 Evaluation: Signal Quality, Decisions, and Task Success232
No single metric captures the full task. SI-SDR compares an estimated waveform with a reference233
after scale alignment; STOI estimates intelligibility changes; PESQ estimates perceptual speech234
quality under telephone-network/codec conditions. Separation resources such as LibriMix, vehicle235
acoustic resources such as CAVEMOVE and AISHELL-5, and vehicle-interior sound classification236
provide different data conditions rather than interchangeable benchmarks [1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 32–34]. The237
paper’s extraction score is therefore evidence about waveform reconstruction on its CAVEMOVE238
split; the FSC intent score is evidence about the clean intent test split. Neither is an end-to-end239

3.1.2 LibriMix271
LibriMix is an open-source speech-separation corpus generated from LibriSpeech. The recipe for272
mixing involves number of speakers, sample rate, the upper and lower bounds for duration. The273
mixing procedure is as follows: A target speaker and distractor speakers are taken, with random274
utterance and crop, and the distractor gain is sampled from 0.7-1.3. Peak scaling is applied as275
needed, and this generates 2-speaker fixed 4 second mono mixtures at 8kHz from clean LibriSpeech276
data.277
Additionally, for this model, multi channel data is generated with varying gains , and delays,278
thus simulating multi mic conditions.279
3.1.3 AISHELL-5280
This dataset, though created on Chinese multi-speaker speech recorded in a car, it is used in this281
pipeline for real-car cabin noise, and provides valuable domain related evaluation/adaptation and282
speech-rich target-absent recordings with embedded noise.283
3.1.4 CAVEMOVE284
This dataset contains multichannel audio from 3 cars, including impulse recordings, and noise across285
static and moving driving conditions, with several microphone configurations. This is used on top of286
LibriSpeech derived clean mixtures to create more authentic multichannel cabin examples to train287
and test the robustness of the model with. The noise is chosen to add to the mixtures using random288
selection techniques based on SNR values ( -5 to 20 dB). *****289

3.1.6 Fluent Speech Commands294

5 Discussion362
This pipeline, while it reaches high accuracy in mixture to action mapping, only performs well for 3 second data, and363
struggles on long term real time data.364
The target speaker extraction stage performs well, attaining 8.57dB on the mixture and separating the target365
speaker well. This stage requires only improvement in SI-SNR alone.366
In the target presence stage, while the accuracy is high and the pipeline works well globally, it performs poorly367
as the false accept ratio is 96.25% when the threshold is low, and rejects all voice tracks when threshold is high. The368
reason that the pipeline still works is due to the fact that the target speaker block is working well. In future work, this369
stage may be removed (as TSE focuses only on target speech after all) or may use a SNN based network to classify370
presence of speaker in the given waveform.371
In wake word detection, the word used currently is ’Marvin’, in future work a custom wake word dataset may be372
used to train this stage. This stage achieved 96% F1 score and needn’t be improved upon as it is only a small task373
24\ncompared to the other stages. Future work may be focused on either removing this stage and combining with a future374
or previous stage or decreasing the size of the model even further.375
The 31 class intent mapper proves to be the biggest bottleneck in real-time pipelines as the model contains 96376
million parameters. Real time systems require around 5-20 million parameters, and thus having such a large model377
may easily cause large lag in production scenarios. Even though the performance of the model in accuracy is high,378
the trade-off is the large processing time on edge devices, and the fact that it also requires large memory. Future work379
will focus primarily on this stage, creating a smaller, efficient model whose performance in accuracy remains similar,380
but whose memory and time performance will drastically improve.381
There also exists a dataset limitation as datasets like AISHELL-5 is made in Chinese and only noise can be382
extracted from it. Also the Fluent Speech Commands dataset is for smart-home command corpus, not in-car command383
corpus. Hence datasets made for English, and made with the context of in-car situations would prove the working of384
real-time car pipelines well.385
